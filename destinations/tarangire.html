Tarangire National Park

About Tarangire National Park

Tarangire National Park, located in northern Tanzania, is renowned for its vast landscapes dotted with majestic baobab trees and its impressive elephant population. During the dry season, the Tarangire River becomes a vital water source, attracting thousands of animals from across the region, creating one of the highest concentrations of wildlife outside the Serengeti.

With its seasonal swamps, rolling savannah, and diverse habitats, Tarangire offers a unique safari experience. It is also home to rare species such as the fringe-eared oryx, greater kudu, and the elusive African wild dog.

Facts about Tarangire

  • Located in northern Tanzania, about 120 km southwest of Arusha.
  • Named after the Tarangire River, which is a lifeline for wildlife during the dry season.
  • Famous for its massive herds of elephants, sometimes numbering up to 300 in a single gathering.
  • Dotted with iconic **baobab trees**, some over 1,000 years old.
  • Home to rare species like fringe-eared oryx, greater kudu, and African wild dogs.
  • Hosts over 550 bird species, making it a birdwatcher’s paradise.
  • Part of the northern safari circuit, often combined with Serengeti and Ngorongoro Crater.
