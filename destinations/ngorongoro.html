Ngorongoro Crater National Park

About Ngorongoro Crater National Park

The Ngorongoro Conservation Area in northern Tanzania is one of Africa’s most spectacular safari destinations and a UNESCO World Heritage Site. At its heart lies the world-famous Ngorongoro Crater, the largest intact volcanic caldera on Earth, spanning about 260 square kilometers. The crater’s fertile floor and permanent water sources sustain an density of wildlife, making it one of the best places to see Africa’s “Big Five” in a single day. Beyond the crater, the conservation area combines breathtaking landscapes, rich Maasai culture, archaeological heritage, and diverse ecosystems.

Facts about Ngorongoro Crater National Park

  • World’s largest intact caldera: Formed about 2–3 million years ago after a massive volcanic eruption.
  • High wildlife density: Around 25,000 animals live in the crater, including lions, elephants, wildebeests, zebras, hippos, and black rhinos.
  • Big Five hotspot: Lions, leopards, elephants, buffalo, and the critically endangered black rhino can all be found here.
  • Birdlife paradise: Over 500 species of birds, including flamingos at Lake Magadi inside the crater.
  • Cultural significance: The land is shared with the Maasai people, who graze their cattle alongside wild animals.
  • Archaeological importance: Nearby Olduvai Gorge is one of the most significant paleoanthropological sites in the world, where early human fossils were discovered.
  • Climate: The crater rim is cooler (about 2,200m above sea level), while the crater floor is warmer and more humid.

Activities

Game Drives in the Crater

Experience one of the world’s best safari drives. Spot lions, elephants, buffalo, wildebeest, zebras, hippos, and the elusive black rhino. Photographers love the crater’s unique backdrop of steep walls and lush landscapes.

Brdwatching

Over 500 bird species, from ostriches and crowned cranes to flamingos at Lake Magadi. Raptors such as augur buzzards and bateleur eagles can also be seen.

Visit Olduvai Gorge

Explore the “Cradle of Mankind,” where fossils of early humans dating back nearly 2 million years were discovered. Visit the Olduvai Museum for archaeological insights.

Nature Walks & Hiking

Guided hikes along the crater rim offer breathtaking panoramic views. Walks in the Empakaai Crater feature flamingos and lush forest scenery. Hike to Olmoti Crater for waterfalls and scenic landscapes.
