Serengeti National Park

About Serengeti National Park

The Serengeti National Park, located in northern Tanzania, is one of Africa’s most celebrated wildlife reserves. Known for its endless golden plains, acacia-studded landscapes, and rich biodiversity, the Serengeti is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and home to the world’s greatest wildlife spectacle—the Great Migration.

Each year, over 1.5 million wildebeest, accompanied by zebras and gazelles, embark on a dramatic journey across the Serengeti plains in search of fresh grazing, attracting predators such as lions, leopards, and crocodiles. This makes the Serengeti a true safari paradise and one of the Seven Natural Wonders of Africa.

Facts about Serengeti

  • Located in northern Tanzania, covering about 14,750 km².
  • Home to the famous **Great Wildebeest Migration**—one of the largest animal migrations in the world.
  • A UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of the **Seven Natural Wonders of Africa**.
  • Hosts over 70 large mammal species and more than 500 bird species.
  • The Serengeti ecosystem extends into Kenya, where it’s known as the Maasai Mara.
  • “Serengeti” comes from the Maasai word “Siringit,” meaning “endless plains.”
  • One of the best places in the world to see lions, leopards, cheetahs, and hyenas in action.
