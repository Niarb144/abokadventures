Ol Pejeta Conservancy

About Ol Pejeta Conservancy

Ol Pejeta is home to the largest black rhino sanctuary in East Africa and gained global recognition as the last home of the world’s two remaining northern white rhinos. diverse habitats and abundant wildlife, it offers one of the most rewarding safari experiences in Kenya.

Book Destination

Facts about Ol Pejeta

  • Largest black rhino sanctuary in East Africa: Home to over 160 black rhinos.
  • Northern white rhinos: Ol Pejeta is the last place on earth where you can see the remaining two northern white rhinos, Najin and Fatu.
  • Big Five haven: Lions, leopards, elephants, buffalo, and rhinos all thrive here.
  • Chimpanzee sanctuary: Hosts the only sanctuary for rescued chimpanzees in Kenya, the Sweetwaters Chimpanzee Sanctuary, managed in partnership with the Jane Goodall Institute.
  • Conservation innovation: Pioneers in advanced technologies like drone monitoring, canine anti-poaching units, and cattle-wildlife integration.
  • Historic site: Once a colonial-era cattle ranch, now transformed into a global model for wildlife conservation.

Activities

Visit the Northern White Rhinos

Meet Najin and Fatu, the last two northern white rhinos on the planet. Learn about global conservation efforts to save the subspecies through advanced reproductive technology.

Chimpanzee Sanctuary Tour

Explore the Sweetwaters Chimpanzee Sanctuary, home to rescued and orphaned chimpanzees. Learn about primate conservation and rehabilitation.

Guided Bush Walks

Take a guided walking safari for a closer look at wildlife, plants, and tracks. Great for learning bushcraft skills and appreciating nature on foot.

Lion Tracking

Join researchers using radio telemetry to track lions. Help collect data for predator conservation while observing lions in their natural habitat.

Cycling Safari

Unique biking tours through wildlife areas—pedal past giraffes, zebras, and gazelles while guided by rangers.

Horseback Safaris with Rhinos

Ride horses among rhinos in a predator-free sanctuary area. A rare opportunity to safely interact with endangered wildlife.
